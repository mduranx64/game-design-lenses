# Interactive Narrative

Establish what the story contributes to the requested game. Choose the amount and structure of authored narrative that supports the intended experience. A game can work with little explicit plot.

## Design the playable story

1. Align the player's goal, the character's motive, the obstacle, and the action used to overcome it. Surface mismatches where mechanics reward behavior the fiction condemns or where story stakes vanish during ordinary play.
2. Choose the structure appropriate to scope: authored progression with local freedom, branching with controlled reconvergence, systemic events that generate player stories, or a deliberate mixture. State which consequences persist after branches rejoin.
3. Define a decision by available information, alternatives, cost, state change, and later acknowledgment. Distinguish expressive choices from outcome-changing choices so promises of agency match the design.
4. Make obstacles cause action and change. Use archetypal story structures only when they fit; avoid forcing every narrative into the hero's journey.
5. Ground unfamiliar elements in consistent rules and recognizable motives. Concentrate novelty so players can understand what matters. Plan how key information reaches players who skip or miss a scene.
6. Use environmental cues, goals, interfaces, characters, and sound to guide attention while maintaining believable in-world behavior. Account for refusal, detours, repeated attempts, and sequence breaks.
7. Walk a successful path and a divergent path. Check state continuity, impossible dialogue, unearned character change, and production cost from branch multiplication.

## Deliver

Provide a playable beat/quest structure, choice-and-consequence table, important state flags, and a worked divergent path. Keep prose scenes subordinate to the requested design deliverable. When the game is systemic, describe what kinds of stories its rules can generate rather than inventing a fixed plot.
