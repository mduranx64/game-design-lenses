# Playtest Planning and Analysis

Identify whether the task is to plan a test, analyze existing evidence, or conduct an authorized session. An AI walkthrough can inspect rules; it cannot establish how human players feel.

## Plan or analyze

1. Define the design decision and a few specific uncertainties. Replace 'is it fun?' with observable questions about understanding, choices, challenge, replay interest, or another stated goal.
2. Select participants by the question. Fresh players reveal first-use issues; returning players reveal learning and repeated-play issues; experts reveal specialized strategies. Record developer/friend bias and avoid treating one group as the entire audience.
3. Choose a setting and build version that preserve the relevant experience. Define tasks, starting conditions, consistent neutral instructions, duration, and what counts as intervention.
4. Record behavior with time/context, exact utterances when available, actions, outcome, and assistance. Keep observations separate from interpretations and proposed fixes. Avoid coaching during a first-use test; log assistance when it becomes necessary.
5. Collect retrospective explanations with open, non-leading questions. A facial expression can suggest a hypothesis but does not prove an emotion. Players describe their experience; they need not design the solution.
6. Analyze repeated patterns and contradictions, including outliers. Report counts with denominators, limitations of small or selected samples, and alternative explanations. Tie each recommendation to evidence and a verification plan.
7. If there is no participant data, deliver a test plan and empty capture structure. Keep illustrative data visibly labeled and out of findings. Contacting or recording participants requires the authorization appropriate to that action.

## Deliver

For planning: decision, questions, participants, setting, protocol, observation sheet, and decision criteria. For analysis: evidence-linked findings, confidence/limitations, candidate changes, and retest. Do not convert a draft test plan into a claim that testing occurred.
