# Game Pitch and Business Assumptions

Determine the recipient and the decision the pitch should support. If those are unknown, state a reasonable working assumption and tailor the draft accordingly. Preserve the game's identity and keep promises aligned with evidence and production scope.

## Build the case

1. Distinguish what a client requests from the underlying goal the request may address. Present the inferred goal as a hypothesis; do not claim knowledge of someone's hidden motives.
2. Lead with the player experience and a concrete moment of play. Establish audience, hook, core loop, and what makes the proposed experience distinct.
3. Support feasibility with available prototype evidence, team capabilities, scope, milestones, dependencies, and remaining risks. Label mockups, plans, and measured results correctly.
4. Match the ask to the next useful milestone. Explain what resources enable and what evidence that milestone will produce.
5. If commercial modeling is requested, separate assumptions from verified data. For a simple paid-unit scenario, contribution per unit = net receipts per unit - variable cost per unit; break-even units = ceil(fixed costs / positive contribution). Specify what is already deducted so platform fees, taxes, or refunds are not counted twice. A nonpositive contribution does not yield a finite break-even under that model.
6. Use sensitivity cases rather than invented sales forecasts. Fetch current sources for real market comparisons, pricing, or platform terms when needed; do not reuse the book's historical market details as current facts.
7. Adapt length and supporting materials to the meeting. Include likely questions and an honest response to the biggest uncertainty.

## Deliver

Produce the requested pitch format, specific ask, evidence/assumption distinctions, and relevant supporting calculations. Drafting a pitch does not authorize sending it, contacting a publisher, or making commitments for the user.
