# Example: players abandon a route puzzle

**Given:** Three first-time players stop at the route-selection screen. A designer says the puzzle must be too hard.

**Competing explanations:** Players may not understand the destination, may not recognize clickable roads, or may understand both and find the planning difficult. The three observations do not distinguish these explanations.

**Player model:** A proposed audience enjoys short planning challenges and expects reversible choices. Prior map-game experience is unknown.

**Next observation:** Before teaching the controls, ask players to describe what they believe the destination is and what they expect a road selection to do. Record their first attempt. Test a separate returning group for challenge after the interface is understood.

**Candidate change:** Preview a route and allow cancellation. It could reduce fear of an irreversible error, but it might also reveal information the puzzle is meant to withhold. Test that tradeoff.
