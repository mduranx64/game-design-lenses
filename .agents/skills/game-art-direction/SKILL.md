# Game Art and Audio Direction

Translate the experience and theme into concrete perceptual choices. Aesthetics can suggest mechanics and alter how play feels; they are not merely a finishing layer. Keep the user's medium and production capacity in view.

## Establish direction

1. Describe the intended perceptual and emotional qualities with specific contrasts, shapes, movement, materials, sound, and rhythm. Avoid a list of vague adjectives without implementation consequences.
2. Relate choices to the four elements: what mechanics require players to distinguish, what the fiction promises, and what the chosen technology or physical materials can deliver.
3. Separate essential gameplay information from atmosphere. Define a readable hierarchy for threats, opportunities, interaction, and background detail. Test at actual gameplay scale and likely viewing/listening conditions.
4. Give audio a deliberate role: place, action feedback, anticipation, identity, or rhythm. Define what silence does. Provide alternate critical cues when sound is unavailable.
5. Choose where a strong suggestive detail can let imagination fill a gap and where ambiguity would harm understanding. Match fidelity to what can be made consistently; do not prescribe realism as a quality target.
6. Identify a small representative art/audio test that includes the hardest readability or production constraint. Specify what success would look or sound like and how it will be inspected in play.

## Deliver

Produce a usable direction brief with specific principles, examples of application, signal hierarchy, audio roles, production constraints, and a validation scene. Describe unproduced assets as specifications. Do not claim screenshots, audio, or a playable scene were created or inspected unless they actually were.
