# Game Design Lens Review

Establish the artifact, the intended experience, and the decision the review should support. Distinguish a concept review, a rules inspection, a playable-build observation, and a review of actual player data.

## Choose the right scope

Read [the skill map](../../references/skill-map.md) when selecting a focused workflow. If the request already names a specific task, use that workflow rather than expanding it into a full audit. Read the relevant rows of [the lens index](../../references/lens-index.md) when the user names a lens or asks for broader coverage; do not load every skill or apply all 100 lenses by default.

## Review

1. Restate the experience and constraints. Separate supplied facts, observations, hypotheses, and unknowns.
2. Inspect the relationship between mechanics, story, aesthetics, and technology/materials. Find where a concrete rule or presentation choice supports or contradicts the promise.
3. Select a small, justified set of lenses matched to the uncertainty. For a broad review, consider intent, audience, experience, novelty, commercial constraints where relevant, feasibility, social goals, and playtesting evidence. Unknown is not a failed test.
4. For each consequential finding, show the specific artifact evidence or reasoning, the likely player consequence, a plausible alternative explanation, and a bounded change or experiment. Avoid findings that only restate a lens question.
5. Prioritize problems that can invalidate the core experience before optional polish. Preserve what already works and explain tradeoffs when recommendations conflict.
6. End with a next experiment that could change the recommendation. State what remains unobserved; do not present a desk review as human playtesting.

## Deliver

Provide scope, selected lenses with edition-specific references, a short ordered set of actionable findings, and the next test. For an explicit exhaustive audit, track every lens as examined, not applicable, or unknown with a reason; do not manufacture findings to fill a checklist. Refer to book ideas as lenses for judgment, not validated universal laws.
