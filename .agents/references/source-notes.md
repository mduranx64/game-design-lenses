## What comes from the book

The skills apply the book's experience-first approach; the mechanics/story/aesthetics/technology tetrad; problem framing and iteration; player perspective; mechanical state, actions, rules, skill and chance; balance; puzzle construction; interaction feedback; interest curves; interactive story and guidance; worlds and characters; spatial and aesthetic design; social play; team communication; playtesting; technology fit; pitching; and design purpose and responsibility.

All 100 lenses are indexed in [lens-index.md](lens-index.md). Each skill names its relevant chapters and lenses. [chapter-map.md](chapter-map.md) explains chapter coverage. This is a practical adaptation of the book's methods, not a claim to reproduce every argument, example, or exercise in the book.

## What this plugin adds

Task routing, output structures, original examples, explicit assumption/evidence labels, computational walkthroughs, decision criteria, and validation scenarios are the plugin author's operational additions. They are not quotations or claims that these exact procedures occur in the book.

Present-day adaptations include avoiding demographic stereotypes as player evidence; distinguishing cognitive puzzle entry from disability accessibility; using multiple critical feedback channels; identifying online abuse and dropout recovery; labeling dated technology and market information; and separating predicted player reactions from observed human results. These additions are identified in relevant skills or are general evidence-handling conventions.

The book's questions are design prompts rather than scientific guarantees. Do not generalize its historical examples into current medical, psychological, legal, technical, or market claims. Verify current facts from appropriate sources when a task actually needs them.
